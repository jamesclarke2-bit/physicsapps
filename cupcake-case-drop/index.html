Reading the graph

Error bars show half the range of your times. The dashed line is what you would get if every case fell at terminal velocity for the whole drop: since TV² = mg ÷ k, speed² against mass is then a straight line through the origin. Where your data sits below the dashed line, the case was still speeding up for part of the drop, so the assumption (and your measurement of TV) fails there. A longer drop or a lighter case keeps the assumption valid.

What the target means

The experiment works best when the case speeds up quickly, then falls at constant speed for most of the drop. Then average speed (h ÷ t) is almost the same as terminal velocity (TV), which is what we want to measure.

The percentage on the first graph is average speed ÷ TV. 100% means constant speed all the way down. A lower value means more of the fall was spent still speeding up. Set the target you want to aim for with the slider.

    How this simulation works

    The case falls under its weight W = mg and air resistance D = kv². Terminal velocity is reached when W = D, so TV = √(mg ÷ k). The drag constant k = ½ρCdA is proportional to the face-on area A of the case. A standard case of 40 cm² has k = 0.0037 kg/m. So TV depends on mass ÷ area: more mass raises it, more area lowers it. Each case is 0.55 g whatever its area. Real cases will differ, so use the model for the shape of the results, not exact numbers.

    Each timing includes a random error from the method (stopwatch ±0.07 s, phone video ±0.01 s, light gates ±0.003 s), about 3% natural variation from drop to drop, and an occasional anomaly such as a tumbling case or a slow stopwatch press. Because these change from drop to drop they show up as the range of repeated times, and half the range is the uncertainty. The Improve the design checks use the biggest single source of error as a quick first estimate of that half-range.
